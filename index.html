The shift

Physics is now a first-order design constraint

The industry has converged on this. Writing for Semiconductor Digest, Synopsys' Larry Williams argues that stacked architectures, chiplets and 3DIC packaging have made electrical, thermal and mechanical behavior impossible to separate — effects once treated as second-order now govern performance, yield and reliability, and physics has to enter at design time rather than wait for downstream sign-off.

"The physics is coupled."

That is the premise NoveetyAI is built on: in an advanced package, a flow that analyzes each domain in isolation is describing a chip that doesn't exist.

Larry Williams · Synopsys "Silicon Meets Reality: Why Physics Is Now a First-Order Design Constraint for Chips" — Semiconductor Digest ↗

Recognizing the problem is not the same as solving it. Moving physics to design time only works if the physics is fast enough to be called on every candidate — and if something is driving those calls. Sign-off simulators are accurate but far too slow to sit in an optimization loop, so integrity still gets checked last, when it is most expensive to fix. NoveetyAI closes both halves: multiphysics engines built for loop speed, and an agentic flow that acts on what they return.

🕸️

Sign-off doesn't scale

Direct transient solves on million-node grids and full 3D FEM thermal runs cost minutes each. A design-space search needs thousands of evaluations.

🔗

The physics is coupled

IR-drop drives Joule heating; heat drives electromigration and thermomigration; CTE mismatch drives stress and warpage. Analyzed in isolation, each one lies.

🤖

Agents need callable physics

An autonomous design agent can only optimize what it can invoke. GUI-bound, hand-run analysis cannot close the loop.

Running in parallel · engine technology

Future acceleration by AI-native simulation engines

Our engines accelerate physics by restructuring the math — Krylov reduction, spectral decomposition, layer-wise FEM. In parallel we are building engines that learn the physics, drawing on a decade of PINN, CNN/GNN and transformer-based physics modeling at VSCLAB. Each will ship the way the current engines do: validated against a ground-truth reference solver, CLI-first, structured I/O.

Unsupervised
Physics-informed

PINN & PINO

Physics-informed neural networks embed the governing PDEs directly in the loss, so training needs no labeled simulation data. Physics-informed neural operators go further — learning the solution operator that maps power maps, boundary conditions and material stacks to full fields, so one trained model generalizes across designs in a single forward pass.

  • No labeled data required
  • Operator learning generalizes across designs
  • Real-time thermal, stress and IR-drop
Supervised
Learned surrogates

CNN & GNN

Where high-quality simulation data exists, convolutional networks excel on gridded fields such as thermal and power maps, while graph neural networks match the native structure of the problem — power-grid netlists, FEM meshes and chiplet interconnect are all graphs, and a GNN propagates physics along the same topology the solver traverses.

  • CNNs for gridded fields
  • GNNs follow the netlist and mesh topology
  • Transfers to unseen grids and stack-ups
Roadmap
Foundation models

Transformer multiphysics

Rather than one model per physics, a single transformer backbone pretrained across coupled electrical, thermal, reliability and mechanical data, then fine-tuned per task — offering what point solvers cannot: cross-physics transfer, few-shot adaptation to new packages and materials, and a common representation an LLM orchestrator can query directly.

  • Cross-physics transfer
  • Few-shot adaptation to new packages
  • Native fit for agentic orchestration

Physics is the gate

No matter how fast the flow becomes, or how much of it an agent drives, hardware is ultimately limited and gated by fundamental physics. Signal integrity, power and thermal integrity, electromigration and thermal-mechanical aging are not soft objectives an optimizer can trade away — they decide whether a part works at all, and whether it still works years later in the field.

That is the role NoveetyAI's sign-off capability plays in an agentic EDA flow. An agent may explore thousands of candidates with fast surrogates, but every candidate it advances is checked against physics-grade engines validated on a ground-truth reference solver. Speed moves the search; physics decides what ships.
