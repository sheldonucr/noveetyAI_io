Thermal analysis

ThermStack

From chip, package and board design files to thermal insight, with hot spots resolved on demand down to 20 µm on a full package.
Request access

GPU power maps now carry hot spots of 20–60 µm on packages 50–60 mm across. ThermStack APR (Adaptive Physical Reasoning) keeps the whole package in the model and resolves the hot spots at the resolution you ask for, on one CPU server.

  • Resolution on demand — all eight compute dies of an MI350-inspired package at 20 µm in 56 minutes and 106 GB, where a uniform 20 µm grid is estimated at about 15 TB.
  • Chip, package and board in one model — 3Dblox, GDSII, IPC-2581, ODB++ and ECXML files join into one model with no hand-built geometry, shown on a 974 W MI350-class OAM module.
  • ThermStack-GNN — a package-trained model for repeated exploration: a full 3D map in 10.6 ms at 0.32 K mean field RMSE against FEM, over 360 unseen cases.
  • Numerical fidelity — static and transient temperature fields, with an FEM verification path alongside.

Thermal-mechanical stress

WarpStack

Warpage and corner stress for 2.5D and 3D chiplet packages: resolution on demand, WarpStack-GNN and WarpStack-MOR from one design description.
Request access

A package bows over tens of millimetres, but a die corner concentrates stress over tens of microns. One uniform mesh cannot serve both without becoming unaffordable. WarpStack answers each question at the resolution it needs: the package where warpage has converged, and 50 µm or 10 µm at the corners and gaps you name, in one run.

  • Resolution on demand — 10 µm at five critical corners of an MI350-class package in 5.4 minutes and 27 GB on one desktop, where a uniform 10 µm mesh is projected at about 25 TB.
  • Warpage and stress in one run — warpage within 0.2% of the converged reference, while the compute-die corner reads 5.6× the stress the coarse mesh reports (a severity indicator at a sharp corner, not a converged material stress).
  • WarpStack-GNN — global exploration: full warpage maps in 19.4 ms per design at 0.38% error, fast enough for a live optimization loop.
  • WarpStack-MOR — localized exploration: a compact model over a bounded box around a nominal design, 51 µs per design and 798,000× a full solve.
  • Stack and layout, straight from your files — IEEE 3Dblox or floorplan JSON for the stack, ODB++ or IPC-2581 for the substrate or board. WarpStack reads the copper and drills of every layer itself, so no hand-made copper-density maps, and dies and parts sit on the layout as simple bodies or full 3Dblox models.
  • Hands-off at real scale — a production-class accelerator package imports to 457 mechanical layers. CLI with structured JSON in, CSV/JSON out.

Electromigration & reliability

MetalStack

Full-chip, temperature-aware multiphysics: coupled electromigration, thermomigration and IR-drop over real power-grid networks.
Request access

Black's equation and Blech's limit are over-conservative; even physics-based Korhonen solvers usually assume a uniform die temperature. MetalStack closes that gap. It solves the discretized Korhonen stress equation coupled to an MNA IR-drop solver, driven by real spatial thermal maps — and the results show that where the hotspot sits relative to the critical current paths can matter more than the average temperature.

  • Coupled EM / TM / IR-drop — electromigration and thermomigration evolve together with the electrical network, not in isolation.
  • Realistic spatial thermal maps — accepts external chip-level temperature fields, including real measured profiles, in place of a uniform-die assumption.
  • Joule self-heating — per-node, per-segment wire temperature from current density and path resistance, superimposed on the ambient field.
  • Iterative resistance feedback — void-driven resistance changes are written back to the netlist, so every IR-drop solve reflects the grid's current aging state.
  • Monte Carlo lifetime prediction — TTF distributions, not a single number, across sampled material parameters.
  • Flow integration — operates on power grids from open-source OpenROAD and major commercial EDA flows, retaining via resistances for early-failure assessment.

Why spatial thermal wins

On the RISC-V core, two 353 K thermal maps with nearly identical peak temperature (387.1 K vs 387.6 K) give opposite verdicts — one fails at ~9.4 months, the other never crosses the threshold. Average temperature alone is not sufficient for sign-off.

GridStack

Rapid Analysis of Power-grid Transients via Order Reduction — full transient P/G simulation and dynamic IR-drop on million-node grids.
Request access

Dynamic IR-drop is decided by the full time-domain response of a mesh with millions of nodes, thousands of switching sources, and thousands of time steps. A direct solver re-solves that system step after step — trustworthy, far too slow to keep in a design loop. GridStack never re-solves the full mesh: it distills the grid's dominant dynamics into a compact order-10 rational-Krylov model, marches that through time, and projects back onto your probe nodes.

  • Full time-domain waveforms — V(t) at every probe node under arbitrary switching-current profiles; droop, settling, and dynamic IR-drop step by step.
  • Advanced Krylov subspace reduction — a rational subspace places expansion points for the transient window; an order-10 model reproduces the full response.
  • Domain decomposition — partitions the grid into balanced subdomains solved in parallel across multi-core CPUs and GPUs, then stitched at shared boundary nodes.
  • Full-chip visualization — metal-layer structure, rail-aware 2D IR-drop / VSS-bounce maps, and 3D drop surfaces.
  • Built for sweeps — once the reduced model is built, re-running new current scenarios, decap placements, or floorplans is cheap.
